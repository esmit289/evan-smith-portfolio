PGA Tour Win Predictor

A machine learning model that reads a golfer’s season stats and estimates whether the season looks like a winning one. It trains on the data behind my PGA Tour dashboard and runs as a live API.

I started with logistic regression (ROC AUC 0.763). Its most confident prediction for a realistic dominant stat line topped out near 92%, and one coefficient came out with the wrong sign. I switched to gradient boosting with monotonic constraints, so every stat pushes win odds the right way, and accepted a small drop in AUC. Cross-validated ROC AUC is now 0.747 on 1,973 player-seasons, and only 19.2% of them include a win, so I report balanced accuracy (0.629) too.

Writing on data ethics

Three reflections from my Modeling and Society course, on who is responsible when data shapes people’s lives.

Read the full posts

  • Data science and society

    Data now shapes everyday life, and whoever holds it is responsible for how it’s used. Telling people what their data is for is how you earn their cooperation.

  • When modeling is ethically ambiguous

    Modelers often decide alone whether their work is ethical, and incomplete data or proxy variables can lead to unfair outcomes. I look at three safeguards: talking with stakeholders, making models interpretable, and monitoring them over time.

  • Social determinants of health and capabilities

    Medical measures like A1C are shaped by non-medical factors such as diet and lifestyle, so analysts have to account for what the data doesn’t measure directly. The Capabilities Approach makes the same point about well-being and freedom.
